VTO Model Eval

Five OpenRouter image models, 20 person × garment pairs, 100 outputs. Each output was graded 1–5 on five dimensions and marked usable or unusable. Grading was blind: outputs were shuffled and labelled A–E, and the model key was opened only after all 100 grades were recorded.

Findings

Best value

Seedream 5.0 Flash

19 of 20 usable at $0.018, the lowest cost per usable image. It's generally available and reproduced garment details well. Its one unusable output turned a man around because the product shot showed the back of the tee, and a prompt fix should cover that. Its other weakness is square output on 4:5 photos, which twice distorted body shape.

Best quality

Hy Image 3.5 Preview

The only model with 20 of 20 usable, and a perfect 5.00 on identity. At $0.024 and about 17s it costs about the same as Seedream. It's a preview release, so re-test it at general availability.

Not recommended

FLUX.3, Gemini Flash Lite, Klein

FLUX.3 matches Hy on quality but takes 70s and refused a sheer blouse as adult content. Gemini is the fastest (7s) but changed the person's pose, framing or body in 10 of 20 outputs, and replaced one person entirely. Klein is cheap and fast but only 14 of 20 were usable, because it copies extra clothing from the product photo.

What each model got wrong

Seedream 5.0 Flash

  • pair01: turned the man to face away, copying the back view of the tee.
  • Returns square images for 4:5 photos. In pair14 and pair16 the reframing also changed the body, making it heavier in one and slimmer in the other.
  • Otherwise reproduced garment details faithfully: logos, the corduroy collar, lace, embroidery.

Hy Image 3.5 Preview

  • No unusable outputs, and identity was preserved in every image.
  • Made small garment errors: a blue shirt added under the sweater vest (pair07), the tee print a little small (pair04), jeans a lighter wash than the product (pair09), the red dress slightly orange (pair14).

Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite

  • pair17: replaced the woman in the saree with a different, younger person and added the product photo's skirt.
  • pair11: slimmed the body and copied the product model's pose.
  • Changed pose, framing or body shape in 10 of 20 outputs, for example turning the side-profile woman to face the camera (pair20) and cropping the top of a head (pair19).

FLUX.3 Image

  • pair13: blocked twice as "sexual or adult content". The product was a sheer blouse.
  • pair15: rendered the olive jumpsuit in taupe.
  • Preserved identity and scene almost perfectly, but at 70s p50.

FLUX.2 Klein 4B

  • Added clothing that wasn't the product: a blazer (pair01), a jacket (pair07), a sash (pair20).
  • pair17: swapped the saree for shorts and changed the woman.
  • pair02 and pair13: changed the person's face and body.

Method and limits

Prompt. Every model got the same prompt (v1, in prompt.mjs): image 1 is the person, image 2 is the garment, copy the garment exactly, ignore everything else in the product photo, place it by category, and keep the face, body, pose, background and framing unchanged.

Grading. Claude graded every output, inspecting each image at full resolution where detail mattered: print text, faces, body shape. One grader and one sample per model per pair means small differences of about 0.1 in mean score are noise. The usable counts and the failure types are the reliable signal.

Dataset. 20 Unsplash photos of people and 20 garment photos, deliberately including hard cases: back-view and mannequin product shots, a sheer blouse, a saree, a hijab, plus-size bodies, seated and side-profile poses. Real catalog images are usually cleaner, so production failure rates should be lower.
